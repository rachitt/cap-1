## 1. Problem

Horizon Bank is a digital-first retail bank without a working digital front door.

Its customers cannot open an account, check a balance, move money to someone they trust, or
ask for credit without going through a manual, staff-mediated process. Its operations staff
review loan applications ad hoc, with no queue, no enforced decision reason, and no
tamper-evident record of who decided what and when.

In retail banking those two gaps are not inconveniences. They are liabilities:

- **Money movement without atomicity.** If a transfer debits one account but fails to credit
  the other, the bank has destroyed customer money. If it credits without debiting, it has
  created money. Either outcome is an incident that must never be possible, not an error that
  is merely rare.
- **Decisions without an audit trail.** A credit approval with no recorded actor, timestamp,
  and reason is indefensible under scrutiny. An audit ledger that can be edited after the fact
  is worse than none, because it invites false confidence.

There is a second, unusual constraint. Horizon Bank has mandated that this platform be built
**without any manually written production code**. Every line of application code, every test,
and every database migration must be generated by Claude Code agents under human supervision.
The human's role is architect, supervisor, and quality gatekeeper — writing specifications,
configuring agents, installing guardrails, and reviewing pull requests. They do not type
production code.

This changes the nature of the engineering problem. The difficulty moves from *writing correct
code* to *specifying, constraining, and verifying* code that something else writes. Leverage
comes from the substrate: specifications precise enough to generate from, architecture rules
expressed as executable tests rather than conventions, hooks that reject bad writes at the
tool-call boundary, and an evaluator loop that ratchets quality forward and never lets it slip
back.

## 2. Target Users

### Retail banking customer — primary

A non-technical individual using a phone or a laptop, unsupervised, usually in a short session
with a single goal in mind. They want to know their balance is right, send money to a payee
they have already vetted, and find out whether their loan was approved.

Their tolerance for ambiguity around money is effectively zero. A transfer must either
complete fully or fail completely, and when it fails they must be told plainly why. "Something
went wrong" is not an acceptable outcome when the question is where the money went.

Journeys: onboarding, account management, transfers, beneficiary management, loan application.

### Bank administrator — secondary, internal

Horizon Bank operations staff working a queue on a desktop during business hours. They are
business-technical: comfortable with filters, tables, and status workflows, but not with
databases.

They need to clear pending loan applications with a recorded reason for every decision, and
they need to be able to answer "what happened to this account, and who did it" from the audit
log without asking an engineer.

Journeys: loan review queue, audit log inspection.

### AI-Native Engineer — supervisory

Not a user of the banking product, but the operator of the delivery pipeline. Works through
slash commands, approves sprint contracts, and reviews agent-authored pull requests. Their
working constraints — No-Hand-Coding, Spec-Is-Truth, PR-Only Merge, Synthetic-Data — are
treated as first-class product requirements, not as process preferences.

## 3. Value Proposition

**For customers:** a self-service digital channel where money movement is provably atomic and
every outcome is unambiguous. Balances are exact fixed-point values, never floating-point
approximations. A failed transfer leaves the balance exactly as it was.

**For the bank's operations staff:** a queue-based loan workflow that makes decisions
traceable by construction. A reason is mandatory on every approval and rejection, the state
machine refuses illegal transitions, and every transition is written to an append-only ledger
alongside its actor and timestamp.

**For the bank's risk and compliance posture:** an audit ledger that cannot be rewritten. It is
enforced at three independent levels — the repository exposes no update or delete method, the
database raises on `UPDATE` and `DELETE` against the audit table, and a write-time hook rejects
any code change that attempts either. Defeating the guarantee requires defeating all three.

**For the bank's engineering organisation:** a demonstration that a regulated-domain
application can be delivered end to end by AI agents while quality, security, and architecture
remain fully under human control — because that control is expressed as executable constraints
rather than as review-time vigilance.

## 4. Domain Rules

The business rules that define correct behaviour. Each is implemented as a pure, dependency-free
policy under `backend/src/domain/` and is unit-tested in isolation.

### 4.1 Money

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-01 | All monetary values are fixed-point decimals with a scale of 2. Floating-point arithmetic on money is forbidden at every layer, including serialisation. |
| DR-02 | Money crosses the API boundary as a decimal string, never as a JSON number. |
| DR-03 | Derived amounts round `ROUND_HALF_UP`. Amounts entered by a customer are exact and are rejected if they carry more than two decimal places, rather than being silently rounded. |
| DR-04 | The browser formats money. It never performs arithmetic on it. |

### 4.2 Onboarding and identity

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-05 | A customer registers with credentials and profile details and begins in `PENDING_KYC`. |
| DR-06 | KYC must be completed and verified before login is activated. An unverified customer is refused with a distinct "pending KYC" response, not a generic authentication failure. |
| DR-07 | The brief specifies no age, date-of-birth, or other eligibility criteria for registration. None are imposed. |
| DR-08 | KYC verification is stubbed but deterministic: a well-formed document verifies, and one reserved synthetic document number always rejects, so the failure path stays testable. |
| DR-09 | There are two roles, `CUSTOMER` and `ADMIN`. Administrators are provisioned by the seed script. There is no self-service route to administrator privileges. |

### 4.3 Accounts

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-10 | A newly opened savings account initialises its balance to exactly `0.00`. |
| DR-11 | There is **no minimum balance requirement.** A minimum-balance rule would contradict DR-10 and is deliberately excluded. |
| DR-12 | A customer may read only their own accounts, statements, beneficiaries, and loans. |
| DR-13 | Statements are ordered most-recent-first and are always paginated. |

### 4.4 Transfers

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-14 | A transfer amount must be strictly positive and carry at most two decimal places. |
| DR-15 | A customer may not transfer to their own account. |
| DR-16 | The destination account must exist and be active. |
| DR-17 | Beneficiaries are a convenience feature for saving and reusing payees (`AC-06`). A transfer does **not** require the destination to be a saved beneficiary; `AC-04` permits a transfer to any valid active account. |
| DR-18 | A transfer whose amount exceeds the source balance fails with `InsufficientFundsException`. No partial state is written. |
| DR-19 | The brief specifies no transfer limits, caps, or velocity controls. None are imposed. |
| DR-20 | Debit, credit, transaction records, and the audit entry commit together in a single transaction, under a row lock on the source account, or not at all. |
| DR-21 | Notification dispatch is decoupled from the transfer transaction. A dispatch failure never rolls back a committed transfer. |
| DR-22 | Failed transfer attempts are recorded and audited. They move no money. |

### 4.5 Loans

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-23 | A loan progresses `APPLIED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED or REJECTED → DISBURSED`. Any other transition is refused. |
| DR-24 | The brief states no loan eligibility criteria. Application input is validated for structural validity only (principal positive and at most two decimal places, tenure a positive whole number of months). No income, amount, or ratio thresholds are invented; the approve/reject decision rests entirely with the administrator under `AC-08`. |
| DR-25 | Approval and rejection both require a non-empty reason. An administrator cannot decide silently. |
| DR-26 | Every state transition is appended to an immutable transition history recording the actor, the reason, and the timestamp. |
| DR-27 | Disbursement credits an existing active savings account belonging to the applicant. |

### 4.6 Audit and notification

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-28 | The audit ledger is append-only. No update path and no delete path exists, at any level. |
| DR-29 | Every audit entry records timestamp, actor, action, entity type, entity reference, and the request correlation ID. |
| DR-30 | A notification is raised on every successful transfer and on every loan state transition, without exception. |
| DR-31 | Notifications are persisted to a table **and** emitted as a structured log line, so the behaviour is both assertable in tests and visible to administrators. |

### 4.7 Security and observability

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| DR-32 | Card numbers, passwords, tokens, and KYC document numbers never reach a log line. |
| DR-33 | Authentication is enforced at the API layer. No customer or administrator route is anonymously reachable. |
| DR-34 | Every request carries a correlation ID through structured JSON logs, from middleware entry to audit entry. |
| DR-35 | Database migrations are append-only. A shipped migration is never edited. |
| DR-36 | Only synthetic data is used. No real, confidential, or customer-sensitive data enters this system at any point. |

## 6. Cost of Not Solving

| Dimension | Cost |
|---|---|
| Functional | No digital channel. Customers cannot transact. Credit decisions stay untracked. |
| Financial integrity | Non-atomic transfers risk creating or destroying customer money — the most severe defect class in banking. |
| Compliance | Without an append-only ledger, the bank cannot evidence what happened to customer money or why credit was granted. |
| Operational | Loan review has no queue, no enforced reason, and no traceability, so decisions cannot be reviewed or defended. |
| Programme | The capstone is graded on a 21-parameter, 100-mark rubric. Below 60 is Not Yet Passed, and a single missing mandatory deliverable can cost a full grade band. |

## 7. Scope Boundary

**Delivered:** onboarding with a KYC stub, savings accounts, balance and statements, atomic
transfers, beneficiary management, personal loans with a full state machine, an administrator
review queue, an append-only audit ledger, stub notifications on transfer success and every
loan state transition, a responsive React front end, and the complete AI-native engineering
substrate that produced all of it.

**Not delivered:** real payment rails or interbank settlement, real KYC document verification,
real SMS or email gateways, production deployment, secret management, multi-region or
distributed locking, penetration testing, threat modelling, and regulatory filings. All are
explicitly out of scope for this engagement.

Full scope detail, alternatives considered, edge cases, assumptions, and the traceability
matrix are in `specs/brd/brd.md`.
