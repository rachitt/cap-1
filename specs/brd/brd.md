## 1. Executive Summary

Horizon Bank, a digital-first retail bank, needs a modernized customer-facing banking
platform. The platform must support the full retail journey: onboarding with a KYC check,
savings accounts, atomic money transfers between accounts, beneficiary management, personal
loan applications with an admin review queue, an append-only audit ledger, and notifications
on financially significant events.

The binding constraint is not functional. It is **methodological**: no production code may be
written by hand. Every line of application code, every test, and every database migration must
be generated by Claude Code agents operating under human supervision. The human acts as
architect, supervisor, and quality gatekeeper — writing specifications, configuring agents,
installing guardrails, and reviewing pull requests.

The delivered artifact is therefore two things at once:

1. A **working banking application** that satisfies ten functional acceptance criteria
   (`AC-01`..`AC-10`) and eight non-functional requirements (`NFR-01`..`NFR-08`).
2. An **evidence trail** — specs, agent definitions, skills, hooks, commands, sprint
   contracts, evaluator outputs, post-mortems, and PR history — proving the work was
   agent-led rather than hand-written.

Both are graded. The evidence trail carries the larger weight (see Section 4).

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The stated business problem

Horizon Bank's customer-facing platform does not exist in a modern form. Customers cannot
self-onboard, open accounts, move money, manage payees, or apply for credit through a digital
channel. Bank staff have no queue-based workflow for reviewing loan applications and no
tamper-evident record of what happened to customer money and credit decisions.

In a regulated retail banking context, the absence of an append-only audit ledger and of
atomic money movement is not a feature gap — it is an operational and compliance liability.
A transfer that debits without crediting, or a loan approval with no recorded actor and
reason, is an incident.

### 2.2 The real constraint

The bank has mandated that this platform be built without manual coding. This transforms an
ordinary engineering problem into an AI-native engineering problem: the difficulty moves from
*writing correct code* to *specifying, constraining, and verifying* code that an agent writes.
The engineer's leverage comes from the substrate — specifications precise enough to generate
from, architecture rules enforced as executable tests, hooks that block bad writes at the
tool-call boundary, and an evaluator loop that ratchets quality forward and never backward.

### 2.3 Cost of not solving

| Dimension | Cost |
|---|---|
| Functional | No digital channel; customers cannot transact; loan decisions remain untracked and unauditable. |
| Compliance | No append-only ledger means no defensible record of money movement or credit decisions. |
| Financial integrity | Non-atomic transfers risk money creation or destruction — the most severe class of banking defect. |
| Programme | The capstone is graded against a 21-parameter / 100-mark rubric. Falling below 60 is Not Yet Passed. Missing a single Mandatory deliverable (Section 8.1 of the brief) can cost an entire grade band. |

## 6. MVP Definition

The MVP is the smallest deployable slice that satisfies **all ten** acceptance criteria. Because
the ACs are the grading contract, none can be deferred. The MVP is therefore sliced by
*sequence*, not by *subset*: four sprint groups, each landing as an agent-reviewed PR.

### 6.1 Sprint groups

| Group | ID | Covers | Depends on | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | `auth` | `AC-01` | — | Register, KYC stub, activate, login, JWT issuance, role model, `/health`, correlation-ID logging. Establishes the `NFR-04` authentication boundary that every later route relies on. |
| G2 | `accounts` | `AC-02`, `AC-03`, `AC-09` (foundation) | G1 | Savings account creation at `0.00`, balance retrieval, paginated statements, and the append-only audit ledger module. |
| G3 | `payments` | `AC-04`, `AC-05`, `AC-06`, `AC-10` (transfer half) | G2 | Beneficiary CRUD, atomic transfer with `InsufficientFundsException`, notification module. |
| G4 | `loans` | `AC-07`, `AC-08`, `AC-09` (viewer), `AC-10` (loan half) | G3 | Loan application, state machine, admin review queue with mandatory reason, admin audit viewer, notification on every state transition. |

### 6.2 Cross-cutting module placement (circular-dependency avoidance)

Audit and notification are consumed by nearly everything and would create a cycle if treated
as peer features. They are therefore built as **cross-cutting service modules**, not as sprint
groups:

- **Audit module** is built in **G2** and consumed by G3 and G4. It depends only on Types and
  Repository. Nothing in the audit module depends on transfers or loans; callers pass a
  generic `(actor, action, entity_type, entity_id, metadata)` tuple. This breaks the apparent
  "audit needs transactions / transactions need audit" cycle.
- **Notification module** is built in **G3** and consumed by G3 and G4. It depends only on
  Types and Repository, and is invoked by the Service layer through an event-shaped interface.

### 6.3 MVP exit criteria

The MVP is complete when all of the following hold simultaneously:

1. `./init.sh` brings up backend and frontend from a clean checkout, migrates, seeds, and both
   health checks report OK.
2. All ten ACs pass, each with at least one test carrying its `AC-NN` identifier.
3. All eight NFRs are enforced by a hook, a structural test, or a unit test — not by convention.
4. Coverage is at or above 80% with the artefact committed.
5. At least three `--no-ff` merges exist in `main` and zero direct commits.
6. `.gitlab-ci.yml` passes on `main`.

## 7. Alternatives Considered

Three implementation approaches were evaluated during Dimension 2.5 of the interview.

### 7.1 Option A — Layered modular monolith (CHOSEN)

FastAPI backend organised as `backend/src/{types,config,domain,repository,service,api}`, a React
SPA in `frontend/`, SQLite for persistence, both started by a single `./init.sh`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Advantages | Matches the pre-installed layer map in `.claude/architecture.md` and the `check-architecture.js` hook exactly. SQLite provides real transactional atomicity for `AC-04` with zero infrastructure. Satisfies the "single command" mandatory deliverable. Fastest route to >= 3000 lines of *meaningful* code within 20 hours. Already encoded in `project-manifest.json` and `init.sh`. |
| Disadvantages | Domain types are expressed twice (Pydantic and TypeScript), creating a drift risk. SQLite write concurrency is limited — immaterial at this scale, but it means the concurrency test asserts serialisation rather than parallel throughput. |
| Mitigation | Generate TypeScript types from the FastAPI OpenAPI schema rather than hand-maintaining them, so drift is structurally impossible. |
| Best for | A 20-hour budget against an already-fixed manifest and hook configuration. |

**Blend adopted from Option C:** `backend/src/domain/` is a **dependency-free policy package**,
a Layer-1 sibling of `types` rather than a subordinate of `service`. Business rules become pure
functions over value objects — trivially unit-testable, individually coverable, and visibly
separated from orchestration. This directly satisfies the brief's requirement for at least four
rule/policy/validator files under `src/domain/`.

### 7.2 Option B — Server-rendered monolith (REJECTED)

FastAPI with Jinja/HTMX templates and no separate frontend project.

| | |
|---|---|
| Advantages | Roughly half the surface area. No type duplication. Fastest route to 10/10 ACs in isolation. |
| Disadvantages | **Forfeits marks.** Brief §7.2 explicitly requires "Frontend evidence (any of: React / Angular / Vue) with at least one responsive layout." Also contradicts `project-manifest.json` and the frontend branches already present in `init.sh`. |
| Rejection rationale | Optimises for a metric the rubric does not reward, at the cost of a metric it does. |

### 7.3 Option C — Split services (REJECTED)

Separate auth service and core banking service, communicating over HTTP.

| | |
|---|---|
| Advantages | Demonstrates genuine service boundaries; produces a more impressive architecture diagram. |
| Disadvantages | Breaks `AC-04`'s atomicity guarantee across a network boundary, forcing either a distributed transaction or a saga with compensating entries. Doubles CI, health-check, and orchestration work. Brief §6.3 explicitly places distributed locking out of scope. Consumes hours that earn zero rubric marks. |
| Rejection rationale | The single most important correctness requirement in the system (atomic debit + credit) becomes materially harder for no grading benefit. |
| Retained element | The clean domain boundary — realised in-process as `src/domain/` (see 7.1). |

## 8. Technical Architecture

### 8.1 Stack

Fixed by `/Users/rachit/Documents/capstone-1/project-manifest.json`. The brief states the stack
does not affect evaluation, so no time is spent re-litigating it.

| Layer | Choice | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Backend | Python 3.12 + FastAPI | Native `Decimal` support for `NFR-01`; OpenAPI generation for free; Pydantic gives validation at the API boundary. |
| Package manager | `uv` | Fast, lockfile-based, reproducible in CI. |
| Lint / types | `ruff`, `mypy` (strict) | Enforced by `lint-on-save` and `typecheck` hooks on every write. Zero untyped defs. |
| Backend tests | `pytest` + `pytest-cov` | Coverage artefact required by M-05 / M-08. |
| Frontend | React + TypeScript + Vite | Satisfies brief §7.2 frontend evidence requirement. |
| Frontend tests | `vitest` + Testing Library | Component-level coverage. |
| E2E | Playwright, driven via Playwright MCP | Mandatory deliverable #7; evaluator-driven. |
| Database | SQLite | Real ACID transactions for `AC-04`; zero-infrastructure local run. |
| Migrations | Alembic, **append-only** (`NFR-05`) | Shipped migrations are never edited. |
| CI | **GitLab CI** (`.gitlab-ci.yml`) with Claude Code Action wired in | Matches the brief's Virtusa GitLab submission target. |
| Deployment | Local, `./init.sh` | Brief §6.3 excludes production deployment. |

### 8.2 Layered architecture

Dependencies flow downward only. Enforced at write time by
`.claude/hooks/check-architecture.js` and at test time by `tests/architecture/`, satisfying
`NFR-08` ("architecture rules enforced as automated tests, not conventions").

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  UI          React components, pages, state  │  Layer 6
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  API         Routers, DTOs, middleware, auth │  Layer 5
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Service     Orchestration, transactions     │  Layer 4
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Repository  Persistence, queries            │  Layer 3
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Config      Settings, constants, flags      │  Layer 2
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Types  +  Domain    Models · pure policies  │  Layer 1
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

`domain/` is a Layer-1 sibling of `types/`: it may import `types` only, and must import nothing
else. Controllers never call repositories directly — a rule with a dedicated structural test.

### 8.4 Authentication and authorization

| Decision | Value |
|---|---|
| Mechanism | JWT bearer, access token only, stateless |
| Lifetime | 30 minutes |
| Roles | `CUSTOMER`, `ADMIN` |
| Admin provisioning | Seeded by `backend/scripts/seed.py`. No self-service admin registration. |
| Enforcement point | API layer dependency, per `NFR-04`. No anonymous customer or admin route. |
| Ownership checks | Service layer verifies the principal owns the referenced account, beneficiary, or loan; returns 403, never 404-as-403 leakage on owned-resource probes. |
| Secrets | Signing key from environment via the Config layer; never committed. `protect-env` and `detect-secrets` hooks enforce this. |

### 8.5 Money handling (`NFR-01`)

| Decision | Value |
|---|---|
| In-memory type | Python `Decimal`. **Never `float`, anywhere, at any layer.** |
| Storage | SQLite `NUMERIC`, with SQLAlchemy `Numeric(precision=18, scale=2)` and a Decimal-preserving type decorator |
| Scale | 2 decimal places |
| Rounding | `ROUND_HALF_UP`, defined for any future derived amount. No AC requires a derived money value — loans end at `DISBURSED` with no repayment schedule (`A-04`) — so no derivation exists today. Transfer amounts are exact and rejected if they carry more than 2 decimal places. |
| JSON serialisation | Decimal string (`"1234.56"`), never a JSON number — avoids IEEE-754 corruption in transit |
| Frontend | Parsed and formatted as a fixed-point string. No arithmetic on money in the browser. |
| Enforcement | New `money-precision-check` hook flags `float(`, `%f`, and float-typed money annotations at write time. |

### 8.6 Transfer atomicity (`AC-04`, `AC-05`)

A single SQLite transaction encloses the entire operation:

1. Begin transaction; acquire a row lock on the source account.
2. Re-read the source balance inside the lock.
3. Evaluate `transfer_policy` — positive amount, 2dp maximum, not self, destination
   exists and is active, sufficient funds.
4. On failure, raise `InsufficientFundsException` (or the relevant policy exception) and
   **roll back**. No partial write, no audit entry for a non-event, no notification.
5. On success, debit source, credit destination, write the transaction record, append the
   audit entry, and enqueue the notification — **all within the same commit**.
6. Commit.

Notification *dispatch* is decoupled from notification *enqueue*: a dispatch failure must never
roll back a successfully committed transfer.

### 8.7 Audit ledger (`NFR-02`)

Append-only, enforced at three levels so it cannot be defeated by a single mistake:

1. **Repository level** — the audit repository exposes `append()` and read methods only. No
   `update` or `delete` method exists.
2. **Database level** — SQLite triggers raise on `UPDATE` and `DELETE` against `audit_entries`.
3. **Hook level** — a new `audit-immutability-check` hook rejects any write introducing an
   `UPDATE`/`DELETE` statement or ORM mutation targeting the audit table.

### 8.8 Observability (`NFR-03`, `NFR-06`, `NFR-07`)

- Structured JSON logs on every request, carrying a correlation ID generated at middleware
  entry and propagated through the Service layer.
- A redaction filter strips card numbers, passwords, and tokens before emission (`NFR-03`),
  reinforced by a new `pii-scrub` hook at write time.
- `/health` performs a trivial DB round-trip and returns 200 within 1s of successful startup
  (`NFR-07`); the evaluator probes it with 5 retries at 2s backoff.

### 8.9 Domain policy files (`backend/src/domain/`)

Four files minimum, all pure and dependency-free. Every rule below was checked against
`AC-01`..`AC-10` for conflicts before inclusion.

| File | Rules | AC linkage |
|---|---|---|
| `money.py` | Decimal construction, 2dp validation, `ROUND_HALF_UP`, non-negative and positive-amount guards, safe parse/format | `NFR-01`, all money ACs |
| `kyc_policy.py` | Document type and format validation; deterministic stub verdict (well-formed → `VERIFIED`; reserved synthetic document number → `REJECTED`, so the failure path is testable); login blocked until `VERIFIED` | `AC-01` |
| `transfer_policy.py` | Amount > 0 and <= 2dp; no self-transfer (`AC-04` says "another account"); destination account exists and is active; sufficient funds → else `InsufficientFundsException`. No transfer cap: the brief specifies none. | `AC-04`, `AC-05` |
| `loan_eligibility_policy.py` | Structural validation only — principal > 0 and <= 2dp, tenure a positive whole number of months. The brief states no eligibility criteria, so no income, amount, or ratio thresholds are invented; the decision rests with the administrator (`AC-08`). Legal state-transition matrix for `APPLIED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED/REJECTED → DISBURSED`; mandatory non-empty reason on approve and reject | `AC-07`, `AC-08` |

**Explicitly excluded rule:** minimum account balance. It contradicts `AC-02` (new savings
account initialises to `0.00`). Under the Spec-Is-Truth rule, the brief's AC wins. Recorded in

## 9. Data Model Overview

Formal JSON Schema is produced by `/design` in `specs/design/data-models.schema.json`. This is
the entity-level overview.

| Entity | Key fields | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `Customer` | `id`, `email` (unique), `password_hash`, `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, `role` (`CUSTOMER`\|`ADMIN`), `status` (`PENDING_KYC`\|`ACTIVE`\|`SUSPENDED`), `created_at` | Password hashed with a modern KDF; never logged (`NFR-03`) |
| `KycRecord` | `id`, `customer_id`, `document_type`, `document_number`, `status` (`PENDING`\|`VERIFIED`\|`REJECTED`), `verified_at`, `stub_reason` | Stubbed verification. Document number redacted in logs. |
| `Account` | `id`, `customer_id`, `account_number` (unique), `type` (`SAVINGS`), `balance` (`NUMERIC(18,2)`), `currency`, `status` (`ACTIVE`\|`CLOSED`), `opened_at` | Balance initialises to `0.00` (`AC-02`) |
| `Transaction` | `id`, `account_id`, `counterparty_account_id`, `direction` (`DEBIT`\|`CREDIT`), `amount`, `balance_after`, `transfer_id`, `description`, `created_at` | Two rows per transfer (one debit, one credit), sharing a `transfer_id`. Drives statements (`AC-03`). |
| `Transfer` | `id`, `source_account_id`, `destination_account_id`, `amount`, `status` (`COMPLETED`\|`FAILED`), `failure_code`, `created_at` | One row per attempt; failed attempts are recorded but move no money |
| `Beneficiary` | `id`, `customer_id`, `nickname`, `beneficiary_account_number`, `beneficiary_name`, `created_at`, `deleted_at` | Soft delete preserves historical transfer references (`AC-06`) |
| `LoanApplication` | `id`, `customer_id`, `principal`, `tenure_months`, `declared_monthly_income` (optional, informational only — no rule consumes it), `purpose`, `status`, `disbursement_account_id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` | Status per `AC-07` |
| `LoanStateTransition` | `id`, `loan_id`, `from_status`, `to_status`, `actor_id`, `reason`, `created_at` | Append-only. Reason mandatory on approve/reject (`AC-08`). |
| `AuditEntry` | `id`, `occurred_at`, `actor_id`, `actor_role`, `action`, `entity_type`, `entity_id`, `correlation_id`, `metadata_json` | **Append-only** (`NFR-02`). Backs `AC-09`. |
| `Notification` | `id`, `customer_id`, `channel` (`EMAIL`\|`SMS`), `event_type`, `payload`, `status` (`QUEUED`\|`SENT`\|`FAILED`), `created_at`, `sent_at` | Enqueued in-transaction, dispatched out-of-transaction (`AC-10`) |

**Notification policy (`AC-10`):** written to the `notifications` table **and** emitted as a
structured log line. The table makes the criterion directly assertable in tests and visible in
the admin UI; the log line satisfies the brief's "log-to-file is acceptable" allowance.

## 11. Edge Cases & Constraints

### 11.1 Failure modes to be covered by tests

| # | Scenario | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| E-01 | Transfer amount exceeds balance | `InsufficientFundsException` → 422; full rollback; zero money moved (`AC-05`) |
| E-02 | Transfer to own account | 422 rejected by `transfer_policy` |
| E-03 | Destination account does not exist or is closed | 404 / 422; no partial write |
| E-05 | Zero or negative amount | 422 |
| E-06 | Amount with more than 2 decimal places | 422; no silent rounding |
| E-07 | Concurrent transfers from the same account (double-spend) | Row lock serialises; second attempt sees the updated balance; no overdraft |
| E-09 | Login before KYC verification | 403 `PENDING_KYC`, not 401 — distinguishable in tests (`AC-01`) |
| E-10 | KYC submitted twice | 409 |
| E-11 | Illegal loan transition (e.g. `APPLIED → DISBURSED`) | 409; state matrix in `loan_eligibility_policy` (`AC-07`) |
| E-12 | Approving an already-rejected loan | 409 |
| E-13 | Approve or reject with empty reason | 422; reason is mandatory (`AC-08`) |
| E-14 | Non-admin calls an admin route | 403 (`NFR-04`) |
| E-15 | Unauthenticated call to any protected route | 401 (`NFR-04`) |
| E-16 | Audit write fails mid-transaction | Whole transaction rolls back; no money moves without an audit trail |
| E-17 | Attempted `UPDATE`/`DELETE` on `audit_entries` | Blocked at repository, DB trigger, and hook levels (`NFR-02`) |
| E-18 | Notification dispatch fails after a committed transfer | Transfer stands; notification marked `FAILED`; no rollback |
| E-19 | Customer requests another customer's account, statement, beneficiary, or loan | 403 |
| E-20 | Statement request on an account with no transactions | 200 with an empty page, not 404 |
| E-21 | Password, token, or document number appears in a log line | Redaction filter strips it; a test asserts absence (`NFR-03`) |
| E-22 | Loan disbursement to a closed account | 409 |

### 11.2 Operational constraints

| Constraint | Value |
|---|---|
| Uptime | None. Local development only. |
| Startup | `/health` returns 200 within 1s of successful startup (`NFR-07`) |
| Rate limits | None required by any AC. Not implemented. |
| Concurrency | Single-node SQLite. Correctness under concurrency is asserted by serialisation, not throughput. |
| Budget | 20 hours total, fully available |
| Data | **Synthetic only.** No real, confidential, or customer-sensitive data, ever (Synthetic-Data Rule). |
| Migrations | Append-only. A shipped migration is never edited (`NFR-05`). |

### 11.3 Compliance posture

The system handles PII and financial data *in shape* — names, dates of birth, document numbers,
balances — but exclusively synthetic instances. GDPR and PCI-DSS thinking is **design-informing,
not certified**: it justifies the redaction filter, the append-only ledger, hashed credentials,
and role-based access control. The brief places penetration testing, threat modelling, and
regulatory filings out of scope (`OUT-05`).

### 11.4 Risks

| # | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | Float creeps into a money path through a library boundary or a JSON round-trip | Medium | Severe | `money-precision-check` hook; Decimal-string serialisation; explicit precision unit tests |
| R-02 | Coverage drifts below the 80% floor as volume grows | Medium | High | Commit-blocking gate; `coverage-baseline.txt` regression detection; TDD ordering |
| R-03 | Agents hand-edit production code under time pressure, violating No-Hand-Coding | Low | Fatal to grade | `track-writes` hook; PR review; hand-edits confined to substrate paths |

| R-05 | Architecture drift — a controller reaching into a repository | Medium | Medium | `check-architecture.js` at write time plus >= 3 structural tests (`NFR-08`) |

| R-07 | `superpowers@claude-plugins-official` is enabled in `.claude/settings.json` but is **not installed on disk**, so `superpowers:brainstorming` silently falls back | Confirmed | Low | Recorded in Section 14 (A-10). The brainstorming step was run inline instead. Settings are not modified by any agent. |
